## First maker comment (paste after saving)

```text
Hi Product Hunt — Marma-Crypto is an open-source document-sealing protocol for the gap between a valid digital signature and what a human actually sees.

A PDF signature verifies the bytes it covers, not the visible page. A seal can be transplanted, a flattened copy can lose its signature layer, and a leaked data-room copy usually cannot be attributed to a recipient. Marma-Crypto ties the visible seal and the cryptographic digest to the same vector geometry.

What you can do today:
• Production Linux CLI — seal PDF/DOCX/SVG with a 266-bit payload (256-bit digest + 10-bit recipient word), with seal-page policies last / first / first-last / all.
• Browser demo — zero-upload PDF/SVG sealing and a client-side verifier; production parity for DOCX and the 266-bit payload is via the CLI.
• Leak tracing — a separate recipient canary channel lets you compare suspects when a marked copy appears outside the deal room.
• Open source — Apache-2.0 code, reproducible corpus, 1,800+ sample runs, public anchors and a 5/5 physical print-scan fail-closed check.

What it is not: it does not replace PAdES/eIDAS qualified signatures, notarization or a signer's legal identity. It adds a visible, fragile authentication layer; the PDF writer must preserve at least 8 decimals (9 recommended), and flattening destroys the vector carrier by design.

Research status: the IEEE TIFS 2026 manuscript is submitted; the related preprint is on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23560975). The launch page is intentionally explicit about browser-demo vs production-CLI scope.

If you work with M&A data rooms, legal evidence, CAD/SVG or compliance documents, I would value feedback on the workflow you would actually use. Happy to answer technical questions.
```
