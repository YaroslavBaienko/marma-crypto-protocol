## Title (replace the inherited title)

Fragile Authentication of PDF Signatures via Bit-Exact Quantization Index Modulation on Vector Geometry

## Abstract (plain text, paste as one paragraph)

A PDF signature verifies the bytes it covers, not the page a human sees. The visible layer — seals, stamps and logos — is typically not cryptographically bound to the signed content, so a document can present a valid signature next to altered or transplanted visual material. We study a fragile-authentication watermarking approach that closes this gap at the rendering level: the digest of the signed document is embedded, bit-exactly, into the geometry of the visible seal itself, so the mark a person sees is also the mark that is verified. The carrier is a planar incidence configuration of nine triangles (27 scalar degrees of freedom); the payload uses keyed dithered quantization index modulation over an integer coordinate lattice with an exact IEEE-754 rounding argument, and a disjoint carrier degree of freedom carries a per-recipient identifier for leak attribution. No external QR or barcode symbol is added. We evaluate on a corpus of 60 documents produced by LibreOffice, Typst and Chromium print pipelines, each in native, flattened and print-scan-simulated variants (180 PDFs). On 1,800 stamped PDFs the 266-bit payload is recovered exactly, survives 8-decimal re-serialisation, fails closed at 7 decimals, and any edited payload coordinate is detected; flattening destroys the vector carrier and therefore fails closed. Baselines re-implemented on the same carrier expose a capacity–distortion trade-off rather than a single winner. Two implementation facts are stated explicitly: the PDF writer must preserve at least nine decimal places, and the verifier must parse coordinates as text — drawing APIs that store 32-bit floats destroy a 1.8e-8 pt lattice. The method complements, and does not replace, PAdES/eIDAS signatures.
